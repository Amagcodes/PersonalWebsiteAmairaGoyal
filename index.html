01 / About

Curiosity rarely stays in one subject.

My interest in patterns started with Rubik's cubes and grew into robotics, statistical physics, and quantum information. I enjoy building things, then figuring out why they behave differently from the model in my head.

At Bombay Scottish School, Mahim, I study Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Computer Science and English in the ISC curriculum. Outside class, I work on research projects, program robots, mentor younger students, and spend an unreasonable amount of time with my earphones on.

I'm particularly drawn to quantum communication and cryptography, computational physics, and using mathematical models to understand complicated systems.

02 / Research

Questions I'm working on.

From information access in vendor markets to noise in quantum communication, I use computational and mathematical models to explore how complex systems behave.

Computational social physics · Research manuscript

Agent-based modelling of vendor competition

Built a Boltzmann-choice agent-based model to study competition between local and digital vendors, comparing simulation outcomes with a mean-field approximation. Explored how information access and customer-specific behaviour affect market share.

In a simulated ONDC-style proximity-weighting scenario, local vendor share increased from approximately 17% to 55%. This was a model result, not a measured real-world outcome.

Quantum communication · Research apprenticeship

BB84 quantum key distribution & channel noise

Studied BB84 quantum key distribution during a research apprenticeship at IIT Mandi, with a focus on how channel noise affects the reliability and security of quantum communication.

How I work

When the robot disagrees with the code

Software alone doesn't explain every failure. A sensor can behave unexpectedly, a mechanism can introduce friction, and a perfectly reasonable control command can produce the wrong physical motion.

Working alongside mechanical and electrical teammates has made me more interested in the boundary between models and reality.
